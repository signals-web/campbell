A note to critics.

Welcome—and thank you for taking part. The 115 columns gathered here are the work that, on a re-read, felt most worth bringing back—including pieces from Robert's 1996 Pulitzer Prize portfolio.

Picking your article

Browse by chapter, decade, person, or topic. Click Read PDF to see the original column; if one resonates, click Claim this article. Each piece can only be claimed by one critic—first come, first served. If someone's already taken the one you wanted, write to us; we can usually figure something out. But where you can, look for a piece nobody else has picked. Range is what we're after.

What we're asking for

Not a summary. Not a re-evaluation of the building. Your reflection—roughly 600–800 words that take Campbell's column as a starting point and go wherever you want.

Some prompts, none prescriptive:

  • What does this article say about the moment it was written, and the moment we're in now?
  • What does it tell you about the practice of criticism—what it can do, what it asks of the writer, what it costs?
  • What did Robert's career mean to you, personally or professionally?

No template. Some will write close-readings; others a letter; others might use the article as a prompt to write about a building Campbell never reviewed. All welcome.

Practical bits

The site tracks your progress: claimed → reading → intro drafted → done. Move your status as you go.

If a piece you'd like doesn't have a scanned PDF here, the original is available via ProQuest Historical Newspapers: Boston Globe (1960–) through most university and public libraries.

If you need anything—a scan you can't track down, context on someone Campbell mentioned, a phone call to think out loud—write to Chris at grimley@sendoutsignals.com.
